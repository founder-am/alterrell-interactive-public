<h2 class="ai-h2">The Black Tax at Scale</h2>

<ul class="ai-body">
  <li><strong>Government abdication.</strong> Celebrity giving fills gaps that policy was never supposed to create. The headline obscures the accountability gap.</li>
  <li><strong>Same mechanism, different reaction.</strong> When Dolly Parton gives after Gatlinburg, she is a hero. When Oprah gives after Maui, she is a target. The act is identical. Only the race changes.</li>
  <li><strong>The Black Tax at scale.</strong> Black wealth is treated as communal obligation. White wealth is treated as personal virtue. This pattern is structural, not individual.</li>
  <li><strong>Domicile as obligation.</strong> When Beyoncé was born in Houston, she inherited a debt. When Elon Musk moved to Texas, he inherited no such expectation.</li>
  <li><strong>Owners vs. players.</strong> The people who own the teams, the buildings, the land — are never asked. The people who perform are asked first, loudest, and most publicly.</li>
</ul>

<h3 class="ai-h3">What This Tool Does</h3>

<div class="ai-body">
  <p>This tool documents every major wealth holder across nine disaster-prone states — by domicile and birth state — and whether they were publicly asked to contribute to disaster relief.</p>
  <p>It does not criticize any individual's choice to give or not give. It maps the structural pattern: who gets asked, who gets praised, who gets ignored, and who gets attacked.</p>
  <p>The same act — a wealthy person donating money after a natural disaster — produces radically different cultural responses depending on the race of the giver. This tool makes that architecture visible.</p>
</div>

<blockquote class="ai-pullquote">75% of Hart's challenge targets were Black. 0 billionaires were named.</blockquote>

<p class="ai-stamp">Both figures derived from the eight names in the table above.</p>

<div class="ai-body">
