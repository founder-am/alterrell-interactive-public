<div class="ai-body">
  <p>Fast food isn't just convenient — for tens of millions of Americans, it's the most affordable meal on the table. But the structural economics of cheap food hide a compounding health cost: the salt load in a single value meal can exceed what nutritionists recommend for an entire day.</p>
  <p>This isn't accidental. Sodium is a cheap preservative, a flavor amplifier, and an appetite driver. The industry has spent decades calibrating these levels to maximize repeat purchase. The result is a system where the people who can least afford healthcare are buying the food most likely to put them in the hospital.</p>
</div>
