## Overview

Whenever I start talking about market value and product-market fit as it relates to dating, people think I'm being ridiculous or worse: overthinking it. I'm going to tell you why they're wrong and why this matters for larger bodied Black gay men seeking romantic partnership.

When friends share dating stories and updates, I don't hear many of them ask what could be a sensitive question nor does the topic come up unless I'm discussing it with my other larger bodied friends. And I'm almost always the one to lead that discussion because I'm appalled at how much differently I experience desire now that I'm a larger frame compared to when I was more lean and "fit."

I'd like to take these two ideas together to have a real discussion about body size and dating. Before I get there, I'll tell you that when I was "fit" and more closely matched to conventional gay body standards, I experienced different assignments of my value in different cities. Same body, but different experiences of desire.

Some friends might say "dating is hard for everyone" and "it's really a big city problem." Most of what I hear from thinner, conventionally built men assumes a greater tolerance than many larger men have experienced. And if you want to partner with another Black man, there are realities that get ignored. Just because your friends think you're beautiful and desirable, does not mean that their behavior or social norms are not still fatphobic.

- How many Black gay couples do we see of non-matching: body sizes or degree of "masculinity"? Black gay men might not date their appearance twins as white gay men do, but there are other aspects that seem to be very similarly matched.
- Within your friend group, how many people have hooked up with, shared their excitement to go on a date with, or liked a social media post of someone that is a larger build? I do not mean the out of shape football player build or when muscle men call themselves "fat." I mean someone who actually has to shop plus sized and big & tall.
- Have you ever dated a larger bodied person? Yes, we all have preferences. I am making the point that in a community with preferences, it should be no shock that larger bodied men will have a different dating experience.

I published this as data for you to refer back to when people second guess what you've experienced. I also wanted to provide reassurance that there are places where larger bodied Black gay men can find love as well as tips for "chasers" and other fans of larger Black men.

We deserve to be publicly loved on and shown as partners, and not only enjoyed privately.

## My Story

I wrote this as an actual uncle of four, but also a gay elder. I think being this close to 40 makes me an "unc."

I came of age during the Craigslist, BGC Live, Adam4Adam, and Manhunt era. Grindr and hookup apps did not exist until I was about 24. I had already graduated college and learned about racial preferences from the back-and-forth over email where the same games of headless pictures, unclear racial categorization, and gauges for masculinity existed.

As apps became more prevalent, the "no fats, no fems, no blacks, no asians" era was my entire 20s. In my 30s, I noticed a shift as my frame became larger. My experience was not universally the same. I started to get more attention online and when traveling if I visited specific cities, but some cities were a complete bust. I've lived in Boston/Cambridge, New York City, Oakland/San Francisco, Chicago, and London while also traveling extensively across 35+ countries and 70+ cities within the US and abroad. I've had men want to take me home from the club abroad, and be ignored. I've had the experience of feeling like a 2 in Boston only to be told how hot I am when I spent summers in college in NYC and Chicago, returning each time to the place where I was not desired.

Before we think about age or times changing as the variable to consider, I've lived the A/B test. In college, I'd be overlooked and ignored largely on most apps and at clubs. I considered myself less attractive and felt like I was a 2 on the 10-point scale. Then my rising junior summer, I went to NYC where I was heaped with praise in person and on hookup apps. But back to Boston for junior year and ignored again. The next summer, I went to Chicago where I was told how hot I was repeatedly and sleeping with men I had thought were out of my league. And then back again for my senior year only to realize the problem was Boston.

My "market value" in the city where I went to college was lower than other places even though my (inherent) value to myself was the same. I saw the same effect for my Black gay friends when I returned to Boston and they were not being ignored. As someone who has traveled a ton and studied psychology/sociology, I know these are not dynamics that exist equally in every city.

Our value is not dictated by how attractive others find us, but there are feelings of rejection, being invisible, or loneliness that can impact us because of fatphobia and other factors.

## The Map

*[M-1 cards: the city ranking. M-2: gay population. M-3: Black population]*

As the social scientist that I am, I ran a poll on TikTok. I asked: if you're a bigger black man and you dated other black men, what city did you find a partner in?

I received about 100 responses that spanned 25 cities or so. Although the top cities may not shock you, there are a few important notes to consider.

- The largest city where numbers ought to be in a larger bodied, Black gay man's favor was not the highest count
- The highest percent Black city was also not the highest count of votes
- The highest percent gay city was also not the highest count of votes

Instead, there's an interaction of multiple factors to consider. And before we come to simple conclusions like Atlanta is the Black gay Mecca, we should unpack what makes each city's culture and norms what they are. Oakland is not San Francisco, the same as New York is not New Jersey. The same as the suburbs of Chicago are not Chicago and Houston is not Austin.

In places like NYC and LA, the white men with the most money are often built like swimmers and baseball players. In NYC, I have not met many men who could have played football that work in private equity or finance or management consulting. I have seen Big Bubbas (white, thick men) in the South and Midwest who occupy the highest earning jobs. My thinking is that those men are deemed most prized, and their build is a compass or blueprint for what is deemed high or low worth.

One might argue that higher income means greater access to gyms and more pressure to be "fit." Both NYC and DC are above the national Black median income, but we see a different spread of the data. DC seems a larger body size delta between Black and white BMI while NYC sees a smaller gap.

I've been struck by the few thick bodies I see among my Black American, Caribbean, and West African classmates and colleagues here in NYC. Until you factor in class and income. Back in each of our respective homelands, we see many more partnerships for larger and thicker bodied Black people. But you add in hyper capitalism and a desire for thinness in some cities more than others, and you see a spread that has Chicago and Houston below the national Black median for income.

I also believe we can pinpoint some of the trend we see to places where there is a Black middle class. Places where you can see a diversity of body types in professional roles, excelling and building their families. PG County, Chicago, and Atlanta are notable places with a prominent Black middle class that is quite absent in some of the cities that ranked lower on the list.

I believe where Black people (gay or straight) can imagine themselves financially thriving is where we see Black love that is not clipped by capitalism in the same way as places where there is no Black middle class. Desire is not random, but it does seem to follow the money.

## Hold Your Ground

A few things I hope you do and do not take from the data.

**I implore you not to read the rankings as the place of where you should move.** Instead, view it as places to visit and try out a PRIDE event or low-key weekend.
- You should go test out how that city shows you love and if it's more than what you get in your home location.
- Apps can be alluring to test here, but people tend to gravitate towards who is new especially on a grid where you see the same faces.
- Go out in person and see how many heads turn, if people are nicer to you from the door person to the waiter to others.

**Do not consider compromising your values to fit in.**
- If it makes sense for you to downsize, do it for you. Take that however you want it to mean. I say that without judgement. Your body, your decisions.
- Getting more "fit" and muscular may come with its own challenges. You may even start to second guess if you're now being liked for you or for your exterior.

**You're not screwed if your city was not in the top ranking.**
- The poll I conducted was open to whoever saw it. It had 100 people. In an ideal world, I could ask people to put their BMI and way they identify into a survey and read out more data to you.
- I focused on Black men who want to partner with other Black men. That may not be your preference or your reality, so please interpret the results with nuance.
- Moving to a city for love or partnership is not stupid. You will spend a decade or more with the love of your life and you might only spend a few years at a job. Choosing where you are loved most is really quite reasonable. It is part of why I'm leaving NYC soon.

**Do not believe you have to settle.**
- There are men thin to thicker who will love you at your body size. And you don't have to settle for men who have a fetish or make you think you should be grateful that they chose you.
- Being lonely in a relationship can feel even worse than being alone.

**Try to develop community with other larger bodied men.**
- Big Boi events can be a great space for Black men.
- Bear and leather events can be hit or miss. Some people feel welcome there and others less so.
- Ask friends to come with you as a support for events you want to try out.

## Supporting Us

Here are some tips for our lovers and friends.

**For the chasers and lovers of bigger guys** (who themselves are not bigger bodied):
- Some of you say you like bigger men, but your profile says only into muscle and expect us not to be surprised if you like us.
- Some of you say you like bigger men, but then you use objectifying language that can land differently for different people. Some bigger guys might like how you speak, but others won't. I have always hated when guys tell me they want to rub my belly.
- Stop telling us to not be surprised when you express interest. We love ourselves, but the data and pattern has shown us being ignored. You might be the exception and you should be aware that not everyone we've experienced romantically is inclusive.
- The best compliments I've received were "I find you so sexy" but I've hated "I love how you feel like a weighted blanket." Some of these statements may work after a dynamic has been established, but they can come across very weird when we know nothing about you.
- If you don't have any larger friends, maybe you should see if they might have an issue with how you're approaching men. It might help you realize something you had not considered. And if you have more than 1 friend, see how much variation you find.

**For the friends who love us** (who themselves are not bigger bodied):
- Your larger friend may not want to talk about the fatphobia they face, but I would find it hard to believe that race, gender, and sexuality are things that are forbidden in your friendship in the context of dating. Think about how you can create an open space for them to talk and share.
- You do not need to solution for us. Telling us it would be easier if we lost weight, or suggesting anything when not asked for a solution is not your place. Sometimes friendship just means listening and saying "wow, that does sound hard" or "I hate that this is your experience and I want to hug you."
- Ask your larger friend: what do you experience in dating that you do not think I understand because of how I look or present? Some of what you hear might be hard to hear and some of it could be frustration about the system we operate in and you might catch a stray or two.
- If your friend might need someone to talk to that is not you, you should encourage them (and ask if they want help) to find a therapist or a community that supports them.
